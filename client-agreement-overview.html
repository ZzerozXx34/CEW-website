Client Agreement Overview

Last updated: October 3, 2026

This page explains the commercial framework C.E.W. expects to use for paid engagements. It is an overview, not a substitute for the signed client agreement.

Contract hierarchy: the signed Client Services Agreement, statement of work, order form, or other written agreement controls the paid engagement if it conflicts with this public overview.

1. Managed execution model

C.E.W. coordinates a team of specialists through team managers. The client communicates priorities and provides necessary information, approvals, access, and business decisions. C.E.W. coordinates the execution of tasks that fall within the agreed scope.

2. Scope and priorities

The public service catalog is not an unlimited task list. The client agreement should define the applicable services, priorities, deliverables, expected turnaround, communication channel, coverage schedule, dependencies, and any exclusions.

3. Client access and security

Clients must provide only the access reasonably needed for the agreed work and should use secure sharing methods. C.E.W. should not receive credentials that the client is not authorized to share. Access should be removed or rotated when no longer required.

4. Intellectual property

The client agreement should specify ownership and licensing for final deliverables, source files, client-provided materials, third-party assets, open-source components, reusable internal tools, templates, automation frameworks, and AI-assisted outputs.

5. Confidentiality

Confidential business information, credentials, customer information, internal documents, and non-public metrics should be protected according to the confidentiality provisions of the client agreement. Public website forms are not intended for confidential credentials or sensitive datasets.

6. Data processing

Where C.E.W. processes personal information on behalf of a client, the parties should document the applicable roles, instructions, security measures, permitted purposes, subprocessors, retention, deletion/return, and incident-handling requirements in the client agreement or a data-processing addendum where appropriate.

7. AI and automation

C.E.W. may use AI-assisted tools and automation to support delivery where appropriate. Client restrictions, prohibited data, human-review requirements, and approved tools should be documented before processing confidential or regulated information with those systems.

8. Third-party costs

Advertising budgets, software subscriptions, paid APIs, domains, hosting, stock assets, platform fees, payment processing, and other external charges should be identified in the order or client agreement. C.E.W. does not treat the ability to manage a third-party service as proof that its fees are included.

9. Results and service levels

C.E.W. can commit to agreed work processes and deliverables where appropriate, but business results such as revenue, leads, search rankings, engagement, ad performance, or platform approval depend on factors outside C.E.W.'s control. Any service-level commitments should be written specifically rather than inferred from website marketing language.

10. Recommended agreement checklist

  • Legal names and contact information of both parties.
  • Scope, deliverables, exclusions, priorities, and dependencies.
  • Team configuration and management model.
  • Fees, billing dates, taxes where applicable, and third-party costs.
  • Start date, renewal, notice, pause, and termination terms.
  • Confidentiality and intellectual-property terms.
  • Data-processing and security requirements.
  • AI and approved-tool restrictions where relevant.
  • Client approval requirements and escalation rules.
  • Limitation of liability, indemnity where appropriate, governing law, and dispute process.
